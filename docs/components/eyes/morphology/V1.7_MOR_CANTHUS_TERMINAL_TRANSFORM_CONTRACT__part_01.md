# V1.7 — MOR Canthus Terminal / Closure Representation + Transform Inheritance

**Status:** `IMPLEMENTED — ENGINEERING PASS / DEFAULT VISUAL REVIEWED / REFERENCE PIXEL CALIBRATION OPEN`

## 1. Why V1.7 exists

V1.6 correctly exposed four independent canthus endpoint tangent profiles, but the rendered corner still inherited the older finite-gap cubic closure. When the gap was small, a full contour-width bridge visually became a rounded/vertical cap. That was mathematically valid under the old bridge contract but visually wrong for the intended eye terminal.

A second issue was visible in the V1.6 rotated-gaze artifacts: `rotation_deg` rotated the eye geometry, while the eyebrow remained in an effectively world-horizontal placement. This violated the component hierarchy expected by the current eye builder: a parent/eye rotation must be inherited by its attached eyebrow and other local children.

V1.7 therefore changes the representation rather than adding another curvature scalar.

## 2. Anatomical/semantic clarification

The implementation distinguishes three different concepts:

1. **Medial canthus:** inner eyelid commissure.
2. **Lateral canthus:** outer eyelid commissure.
3. **Lacrimal caruncle:** small medial ocular-surface structure inside the medial corner. It is not the canthus itself and is not present at the lateral corner.

An epicanthal fold is treated as a separate optional medial soft-tissue fold. It is not used to close the eye contour and is not substituted for the caruncle.

This distinction is supported by standard anatomical descriptions: the medial and lateral canthi are both eyelid corners, while the lacrimal caruncle is a small pink structure at the medial canthus. Epicanthal folds are described as folds involving the medial eyelid region and can partially obscure the caruncle.

## 3. V1.7 terminal topology

`CanthusTerminalSpec` exposes:

- `mode="shared_point"` — default;
- `mode="bridge"` — explicit legacy finite-gap cubic representation.

### Shared-point default

Upper and lower lid endpoints at each corner are resolved to one semantic terminal point. No cubic closure stroke is emitted. The renderer therefore cannot create a full-width vertical cap between two nearly coincident endpoints.

The corner is visually owned by the two lid margins themselves.

### Explicit legacy bridge

The previous V1.4–V1.6 cubic bridge remains available for historical regression and deliberately authored finite-gap morphology. It retains its C1 continuity contract.

A shared-point terminal does **not** claim C1 continuity across the corner. The continuity report exposes `c1_bridges_present=False` for this topology so a valid topological terminal is not falsely reported as a bridge-C1 join.

## 4. Endpoint tangent behaviour under shared terminals

V1.6 endpoint profiles remain real geometry controls under V1.7. The terminal point is fixed, while the first/last interior lid samples are modified.

For a shared terminal the handle is scaled by the current opening. This prevents a fixed tangent support from becoming disproportionately large as the eye approaches closure. The existing finite/self-intersection gates remain active.

## 5. Medial caruncle

A new `CaruncleSpec` is owned by `EyeAppearance`:

- enabled/disabled;
- pale pink/orange default colour;
- opacity;
- width;
- height;
- curvature.

The geometry is generated from the medial terminal toward the eye interior, so the same construction mirrors correctly for the two eyes of a pair.

The caruncle is a filled curved triangular detail. It does not close the eyelid contour and is not rendered at the lateral corner.

## 6. Pair-side ownership

`build_eye_geometry(..., side=...)` now accepts:

- `single` — canonical single-eye frame;
- `image_left` — viewer-left member of a pair, with its medial corner toward pair centre;
- `image_right` — viewer-right member, with its medial corner toward pair centre.

`build_eye_pair` and binocular construction pass these side values explicitly.

This makes the medial feature deterministic:

- image-left eye → medial/caruncle side is the right side of that eye;
- image-right eye → medial/caruncle side is the left side of that eye.

## 7. Parent rotation versus gaze

These are deliberately separate channels.

### Parent/eye rotation

`EyeParameters.rotation_deg` rotates the eye's local geometry. The eyebrow is now generated in the eye-local anatomical frame and rendered with:

`effective_brow_rotation = eye_rotation + local_brow_angle`

The eyebrow position is rotated around the eye centre with the same parent transform. This fixes the V1.6 artifact where the eye rotated but the brow remained visually horizontal.

### Gaze

`Gaze` controls the internal ocular assembly. The gaze solver moves the iris/pupil assembly according to the resolved direction and containment constraints. It does not rotate the eye envelope as a rigid object.

The existing lid-follow system may deform the lid margins slightly as an expression/ocular-follow effect; this is not a rigid envelope rotation and the semantic canthus endpoints remain fixed.

## 8. Other child inheritance

The V1.7 audit covers the existing child systems that are expressed from eye geometry:

- eyebrow: explicit rigid parent rotation;
- eyelashes: local direction is rotated by eye rotation;
- eyelid/crease geometry: derived from the rotated eye-local contour;
- medial caruncle: constructed from terminal → eye-centre vector, therefore follows the local eye frame;
- epicanthic fold: constructed toward the eye interior, so it mirrors correctly;
- iris/pupil assembly: governed by gaze rather than aperture rotation.

Full generic component-world-transform propagation remains a runtime integration concern outside this eye-specific brick; V1.7 does not pretend to solve arbitrary scene graph transform resolution.

## 9. Rendering contract

The renderer is allowed to serialize a geometry-owned bridge only when a four-point bridge exists.

For shared terminals:

- upper/lower lid strokes use `butt` terminal caps to avoid a circular cap at the semantic corner;
- no bridge stroke is emitted;
- the outer aperture path connects the already coincident terminal points directly.

This is not a renderer-only visual hack: the terminal topology is stored in `EyeGeometry` and validated by the geometry layer.

## 10. Lash-terminal correction

The previous V1.6 inspection also showed that endpoint lashes could recreate a false canthus cap even after bridge removal. V1.7 therefore keeps a terminal exclusion zone for upper/lower lash roots. Outer lashes begin slightly before the exact terminal rather than forming a circular mass at the corner.

Lower-lash rendering retains a deterministic sparse-lash floor when a non-zero lower-lash specification would otherwise emit zero marks after terminal exclusion.

This correction is intentionally visual/topological: the terminal is owned by the eyelid margins, not by a lash root.
