# V1.14 — Eyelid / Eyeball Surface Coupling

**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** `ENGINEERING PASS / TARGETED + FULL REGRESSION PASS / VISUAL QA INSPECTED`

## 1. Purpose

V1.14 closes the most important defect exposed by the V1.13 visual evidence: the
pupil must be a child of the already-resolved iris/globe assembly. It must never be
recomputed from the eye origin after gaze or ocular orientation has moved the iris.

The brick also adds a 3D depth representation for the eyelid aperture while keeping
the existing 2D eyelid contour as the authoritative morphology/render clipping owner.

## 2. Parent-child contract

The intended hierarchy is:

```text
EyeGroup
└── Eye / Globe
    ├── Iris
    │   ├── Pupil[0..n]
    │   └── iris-owned details
    ├── Cornea
    └── Eyelid aperture (separate morphology owner)
```

For each pupil:

```text
iris attachment
      ↓
child tangent offset
      ↓
spherical child attachment
      ↓
projection
```

A child is therefore translated and reoriented from the resolved parent frame.

## 3. Defect confirmed from V1.13 artifacts

The original V1.13 artifact was not merely stylistically questionable. The stored
coordinates proved that the pupil was generated at an absolute radial distance of
approximately `1.01` while the globe radius was `0.5` in the failing examples.

Example at yaw 30°:

```text
iris  = (0.2200, 0.0000, 0.3811)
pupil = (0.4444, 0.0000, 0.7697)
```

The pupil was consequently not an iris child. This is the reason V1.14 replaces the
absolute pupil placement with an iris-owned attachment frame.

## 4. Globe surface

The globe remains a parameterized sphere:

```text
center
radius
```

The iris is represented as a small spherical/geodesic patch rather than a floating
screen-space ellipse. Its projected ellipse is an emergent result of 3D projection.

## 5. Iris attachment

The iris center is placed on the front globe surface from eye-local tangent offsets.
The offset is bounded before sphere intersection so a pathological gaze request
cannot place the attachment outside the globe.

The projected center stored in `SphericalEyeAssembly.iris_projected_center` is the
projection of the actual 3D surface center. It is not the bounding-box center of the
projected outline.

## 6. Pupil attachment

Pupil offsets are first resolved relative to the iris assembly. They are then
transported along the iris tangent frame and re-normalized to the globe surface.

This produces two distinct cases:

```text
pupil offset = (0,0)
→ concentric child

pupil offset != (0,0)
→ displaced child, still attached to iris/globe surface
```

The child radius is independently bounded by the iris radius.

## 7. Multiple pupil children

`pupil_outlines`, `pupil_attachments`, `pupil_projected_centers` and projection
rotation diagnostics are stored per child. The renderer iterates over the actual
child collection instead of rendering only index zero.

## 8. Spherical patch correction

A previous intermediate implementation double-counted the sphere center while
constructing the projected patch. That produced an apparent lateral displacement.
V1.14 reconstructs each spherical sample from the globe origin plus its radial
normal, so the parent center is counted exactly once.

## 9. Eyelid surface coupling

The 2D upper/lower lid curves remain authoritative. V1.14 additionally lifts those
screen-space points onto:

```text
globe surface
or
slightly larger orbital shell
```

The orbital shell is used only where an eyelid point lies outside the spherical
footprint. It is not mislabeled as eyeball anatomy.

The 2D contour is never mutated by this operation.

## 10. Near/far canthus

The lifted inner and outer canthus points receive camera-depth values. The canthus
with the greater positive camera depth is reported as `near_canthus`; the other is
`far_canthus`.

This is a depth diagnostic, not an anatomical claim independent of camera/view
configuration.

## 11. Caruncle and epicanthus

Existing 2D caruncle and epicanthic geometry can now be lifted through the same
surface coupling object. Their semantic ownership remains separate:

```text
caruncle != canthus
caruncle != epicanthus
```

No edge detector is allowed to promote a line to epicanthus automatically.
