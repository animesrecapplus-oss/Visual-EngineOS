# V1.17 Artifact Audit Before V1.18 Cleanup

## Purpose

The V1.17 generated artifacts were inspected before removal. They are retained here
as historical evidence, not as the current visual acceptance set.

## Useful evidence retained in the repository history

- `alignment_p0__sampled.png`: confirms the neutral spherical child and the actual
  stored lid samples.
- `alignment_p30.png`, `alignment_m30.png`: expose the decisive V1.17 defect: the
  non-zero spherical child projection and the face aperture were not in the same
  visual Y convention.
- `alignment_p60.png`, `alignment_m60.png`: demonstrate that the defect becomes
  severe at larger signed alignment values; the iris can disappear from the fixed
  aperture even though the basis remains mathematically valid.
- `contact_sheet.png`: confirms that the failure is systematic rather than a single
  corrupt render.
- `manifest.json`: preserves deterministic case metadata and the V1.17 claim set.

## Defect confirmed

The V1.17 bridge assumed that the spherical projection still needed a +Y-up to -Y-down
conversion, but `projection3d.project_point()` already returned the historical SVG
+Y-down frame. The second inversion was therefore wrong for every non-zero alignment.

A second limitation was architectural: the face aperture remained completely frozen
while the globe rotated. That made a physically moving iris collide with a static
2D clip instead of allowing the lid margins to slide over the globe.

## Consequence

V1.17 is not used as the current visual baseline. Its useful historical evidence is
superseded by V1.18, which fixes the coordinate conversion and introduces the
endpoint-safe eyelid surface coupling layer.

## Cleanup decision

After this audit, `artifacts/v117_alignment_globe_aperture_coupling/` is safe to remove
from the active checkpoint. The V1.17 contract and evidence remain in `docs/` for
historical traceability.
