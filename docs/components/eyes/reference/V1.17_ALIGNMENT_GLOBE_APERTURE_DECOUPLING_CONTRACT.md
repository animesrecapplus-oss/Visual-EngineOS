# V1.17 — Alignment-Axis Globe / Face-Aperture Decoupling Contract

## Purpose

V1.17 closes the specific failure observed in earlier alignment artifacts: the
alignment-axis channel must not rotate/foreshorten the face-owned eyelid aperture.
The eye is a hierarchy with two different owners:

```text
FACE
 └─ ORBIT / APERTURE / CANTHI      <-- face frame; alignment does not mutate it
       ↕
     EYEBALL                           <-- ocular frame
       ├─ SCLERA
       ├─ CORNEA
       ├─ IRIS
       └─ PUPIL                        <-- all inherit the same ocular basis
```

The contract is deliberately narrower than a general renderer claim. It does not
turn a symmetric sphere into a visually asymmetric texture by inventing features.
If a signed alignment rotation is not observable in a particular projection, the
engine must preserve that symmetry rather than fabricate a difference.

## Convention

`alignment_axis` is the local interocular X axis.

- `alignment_positive`: right-hand rotation about +X; anatomical +Y rotates toward +Z.
- `alignment_negative`: the inverse rotation.
- `alignment_neutral`: exactly zero.

`rotation_deg` remains screen-space roll. It is inherited by the face-owned 2D
aperture and is not interchangeable with alignment rotation.

## Ownership rules

### Face-owned and invariant to alignment

- upper lid margin;
- lower lid margin;
- canthi;
- aperture path;
- epicanthic fold;
- medial caruncle outline;
- brow anchor geometry.

These may still inherit the normal face/screen roll.

### Ocular-owned and inherited from the globe

- spherical globe basis;
- corneal surface;
- iris surface;
- pupil surfaces;
- child surface tangent frames.

A pupil is never recomputed from the face origin after the iris moves. It remains a
child of the resolved iris surface.

## Required invariants

For any pair of requests differing only in `alignment_axis_rotation_deg`:

1. the face aperture coordinates are identical;
2. the canthus coordinates are identical;
3. the ocular basis changes for non-zero alignment;
4. iris/cornea/pupil are resolved from that same ocular basis;
5. pupil points remain on the same globe and child hierarchy;
6. positive and negative signs are independently accepted;
7. no screen-space roll is silently introduced;
8. no prompt parser may translate alignment into left/right gaze.

## Visual interpretation

A centered symmetric iris may have identical projected geometry for `+a` and `-a`
under an orthographic camera. That is not a bug: the two orientations are mirror
related in depth and need not produce a 2D difference without asymmetric surface
features, perspective, or depth-sensitive occlusion.

The QA therefore distinguishes:

- **screen-visible geometry**;
- **3D orientation state**;
- **hidden projected surface samples**.

Hidden samples are never drawn as if they were visible iris geometry.

## Prompt-facing vocabulary

Stable semantic names are available for future prompt compilation:

- `alignment_neutral`
- `alignment_positive`
- `alignment_negative`
- `rotate_globe_alignment_positive`
- `rotate_globe_alignment_negative`

These names describe an ocular orientation request. They do not replace `GazeSolver`.

## Acceptance gate

V1.17 is not promoted merely because the basis matrices are orthogonal. Acceptance
requires clean and sampled artifacts showing that the aperture remains face-owned
while ocular children inherit the globe orientation, plus regression tests covering
both signs, roll composition, parent inheritance and child attachment.
