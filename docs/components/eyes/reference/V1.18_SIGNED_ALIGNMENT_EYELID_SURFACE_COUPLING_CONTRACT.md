# V1.18 — Signed Alignment / Eyelid Surface Coupling Correction

## Purpose

V1.17 separated the face aperture from the rotating globe, but its visual evidence
exposed two concrete defects: a camera-frame Y inversion and an aperture that was
mathematically fixed while the globe rotated underneath it. The result could read
as a detached pupil/iris instead of a changing eye.

V1.18 closes that specific gap without changing ownership semantics:

```
FACE
 └─ ORBIT / semantic canthi
       ↕
   EYELID SURFACE  ← face-owned anchors + globe-following interior
       ↕
    EYEBALL
       ├─ CORNEA
       ├─ IRIS
       └─ PUPIL
```

## Coordinate correction

`project_point()` already emits the historical SVG +Y-down image frame. V1.17's
adapter inverted Y a second time. That was invisible at zero alignment and wrong for
non-zero alignment. V1.18 removes that second inversion.

This is a correctness fix, not a visual style adjustment.

## Alignment convention

- `alignment_positive`: right-hand rotation around local interocular +X.
- `alignment_negative`: inverse rotation.
- zero: exact neutral compatibility.

The globe, cornea, iris and pupil inherit the same 3D basis. Pupil placement remains
relative to the resolved iris attachment.

## Eyelid surface coupling

The canthi remain semantic face anchors. Interior upper/lower lid samples may slide
over the globe through a smooth endpoint-weighted coupling. This is deliberately a
surface-following deformation, not a rigid screen-space rotation of the aperture.

The current default `follow_strength=0.10` is an engine morphology parameter. It is
**not** anatomical truth and is not a reference-derived value. It exists only to keep
the procedural aperture visually coupled without producing a triangular or
self-intersecting opening before human-approved V1.11 calibration.

## Globe scale correction

V1.17 used the full eye width as the globe radius. For the current narrow aperture
this made the front optical pole travel too far vertically at ordinary alignment
angles. V1.18 derives the globe radius conservatively from aperture height and iris
scale:

`max(reference_height / 2, iris_radius * 1.20)`

This remains a spherical globe; it is not a claim about anatomical eyeball size.

## Parent-child invariants

1. canthi stay fixed under alignment;
2. interior lid samples may move continuously;
3. globe/cornea/iris/pupil use one ocular basis;
4. pupil remains attached to the iris surface;
5. + and - alignment remain independent;
6. alignment does not become screen roll;
7. zero alignment reproduces the neutral aperture;
8. non-zero alignment changes the visible eye shape without detaching the pupil;
9. all transformed contours remain finite and self-intersection checked;
10. the correction does not promote any pixel-derived style band.

## QA gates

Required visual cases:

- 0, ±15, ±30, ±45, ±60, ±75 degrees;
- alignment + roll;
- alignment + yaw/pitch;
- bilateral same-sign and opposite-sign alignment;
- sampled lid points and iris/pupil child centers;
- neutral regression against pre-alignment geometry.

A mathematically orthogonal basis is insufficient. A render is rejected when the eye
reads as a detached pupil, a floating iris, a triangular cap, or an unintended
whole-face roll.

## Calibration boundary

The supplied reference image remains qualitative evidence. V1.18 does not infer
anatomical numeric truth from it. Human-approved V1.11 pixels remain the future
calibration authority.
