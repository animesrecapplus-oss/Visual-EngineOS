# V1.13 — Spherical Eyeball + Eyelid/Globe Decoupling

**Status:** `SUPERSEDED BY V1.14 / HISTORICAL IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT`

## 1. Purpose

V1.13 introduces the first explicit spherical ocular surface layer. It does not
replace the existing eyelid aperture with a rotated ellipse. The face/eyelid
aperture remains an independent geometry owner while the globe, cornea, iris and
pupil receive a 3D ocular orientation and are projected into the same image space.

The implementation is deliberately minimal: it is a geometric globe/projection
model, not a claim of anatomical completeness, optical refraction, or a full 3D
head renderer.

## 2. Orientation contract

Four semantic channels are retained separately:

- `rotation_deg` → eye roll around the view axis (historical public channel);
- `ocular_yaw_deg` → horizontal view-axis rotation;
- `ocular_pitch_deg` → vertical view-axis rotation;
- `alignment_axis_rotation_deg` → rotation about the local interocular/alignment X axis.

The channels are composed as rotations on a basis. They are never summed as Euler
angles. At `yaw=0`, pitch and alignment rotation share the same geometric axis;
therefore the implementation does not claim four mathematically independent
degrees of freedom in that singular configuration.

## 3. Ocular basis

The local basis is:

```text
+X = alignment / transverse axis
+Y = anatomical vertical axis
+Z = view axis
```

`OcularBasis3D` enforces unit length and pairwise orthogonality. Rodrigues
rotation is used for each authored orientation operation. Parent and child bases
are composed in hierarchy order.

The generic 2D `LocalTransform.matrix()` intentionally ignores V1.13 yaw/pitch
metadata. This prevents depth orientation from silently becoming a screen-space
rotation.

## 4. Parent inheritance

Runtime resolution walks:

```text
Head → Face → EyeGroup → Eye
```

and composes each component's ocular pose onto the inherited basis. The resolved
basis is exposed as `GeometryTransformResolution.ocular_basis`.

The same basis can subsequently be consumed by iris, pupil, cornea, eyelid and
other ocular children without asking `GazeSolver` to solve scene hierarchy.

## 5. Spherical globe

The globe has an explicit center and radius. The sphere silhouette is generated
from the sphere itself rather than from a screen-space ellipse.

Under orthographic projection, a sphere's silhouette remains circular. This is
intentional. Apparent eye-shape changes are caused by the eyelid aperture and the
orientation/visibility of internal ocular surfaces, not by falsely squashing a
sphere's silhouette.

Perspective projection is opt-in and rejects a camera located on or inside the
globe.

## 6. Iris and pupil

The iris is a circle attached to the oriented ocular surface. Its projected ellipse
therefore emerges from the 3D circle and camera projection.

The pupil is projected from the same oriented surface frame and remains smaller
than the iris by contract.

At a back-facing orientation, the iris and pupil are not rendered as inverted or
mirrored shapes: their surface visibility becomes `hidden`.

## 7. Cornea

The V1.13 layer carries a separate projected corneal outline. This is a geometric
surface marker only. Refraction, optical caustics and physically based corneal
transmission are explicitly outside this brick.

## 8. Eyelid/globe separation

The eyelid aperture remains the clipping boundary owned by the existing 2D lid
geometry. The globe does not rewrite the lid curves.

Conceptually:

```text
FACE
└── EYELID APERTURE

OCULAR GLOBE
├── SCLERA
├── CORNEA
├── IRIS
└── PUPIL
```

Projected internal surfaces are clipped by the aperture at render time. This is a
composition relationship, not a mutation of the aperture morphology.

## 9. Visibility contract

The spherical layer exposes independent visibility states for:

- `near_canthus`;
- `far_canthus`;
- `iris`;
- `pupil`;
- `sclera`;
- `cornea`.

The current canthus states are projection diagnostics. They are not yet a full
3D eyelid/canthus depth solution; that belongs to the next coupling brick.

## 10. Prompt semantics

The existing canonical prompt vocabulary remains valid:

```text
look_forward
look_left
look_right
look_up
look_down
look_behind_right_eye
look_behind_left_eye
look_behind_both_eyes
```

`look_behind_*` is resolved to an ocular yaw of 180 degrees and an explicit depth
projection requirement. It is never rewritten into screen-left or screen-right.

At V1.13, a 180-degree view axis correctly makes the front-facing iris/pupil
surfaces hidden. It does not claim to render an anatomically complete rear globe.
