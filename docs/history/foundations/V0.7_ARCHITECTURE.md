# V0.7 — Binocular gaze foundation

## Objective

V0.7 introduces a pair-level gaze layer without contaminating the eye geometry
solver with face-specific assumptions.

The new abstraction is deliberately small:

- `BinocularGazeSolver`: converts two eye centers + one scene-space target into
  two independent line-of-sight vectors and pair diagnostics.
- `BinocularGazeSolution`: stores both vectors, their angles and the angular
  separation between them.
- `build_binocular_eye_geometries`: applies the same scene target to two eyes;
  each eye still runs the existing `GazeSolver` against its own sampled contour,
  iris and pupil footprint.

## Why this is the next step

The V0.6 solver correctly answers: "where can this one pupil go?"
V0.7 answers the next question: "how do two eyes look at the same thing?"

This is a prerequisite for convincing expressions and later head/face logic.
A face must not independently invent two unrelated gaze directions when a shared
visual target exists.

## Important scope boundary

This is a **2D scene-plane binocular model**, not a 3D eye/optic simulation.
It does not claim to model eyeball rotation, corneal refraction, depth of field,
or anatomical extraocular muscles.

Each eye remains morphology-aware. If the eyes differ in shape/opening/iris/pupil
size, their allowed pupil displacement can differ even though their target is
identical.

## Data flow

```text
Scene target (x, y)
        |
        v
BinocularGazeSolver
   /            \
left vector    right vector
   |              |
   v              v
Gaze(target)    Gaze(target)
   |              |
   v              v
GazeSolver      GazeSolver
   |              |
   v              v
left pupil      right pupil
```

The pair layer does not bypass the existing containment rules.

## Tests

V0.7 adds tests for:

- inward convergence toward a shared target;
- common upward target;
- asymmetric eye morphology;
- angular separation diagnostics;
- invalid target coinciding with an eye center.
